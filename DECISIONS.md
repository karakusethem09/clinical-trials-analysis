# Decision log

Judgment calls made while building the cohort and models, with the reason for each. Superseded rows are kept, marked, so the history of the project stays readable. Rows 18 onward follow an audit of the first version.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interventional trials only | Observational studies do not "terminate" in the same sense and follow different registration rules. |
| 2 | Start date 2008 or later | Registration became a legal requirement (FDAAA) in late 2007; earlier records are a self-selected sample. |
| 3 | *Superseded by #18.* Outcome: terminated or withdrawn = stopped early, completed = not | Withdrawn trials stopped before enrolling anyone; both are failures to deliver a result. Status is kept so they can be analysed separately. |
| 4 | Trials with status UNKNOWN are excluded from classification | The registry sets this when a record has not been updated for two years. The true outcome is not known. This removes 8-25% of trials depending on start year and is a limitation. |
| 5 | *Superseded by #20.* Classification uses start years 2008-2019 only | For later years many trials are still running. Among recent trials the ones already resolved are disproportionately the ones that stopped, which would bias the label. |
| 6 | *Superseded by #23.* Split by start year: train 2008-2015, validate 2016-2017, test 2018-2019 | A random split lets the model learn from the future. |
| 7 | Enrollment count is not a feature | For finished trials the registry replaces the planned number with the actual one. Stopped trials have a median of 19 participants versus 60 for completed ones: the field encodes the outcome. |
| 8 | Number of sites and countries are not features | Sites are added as a trial progresses; withdrawn trials often list none. |
| 9 | *Superseded by #22.* Only prospectively registered trials (registered within 21 days of start) | A trial registered long after it began may already have finished at registration, so "predicting" its outcome is meaningless. Registration delay was the single strongest feature before this filter. |
| 10 | *Superseded by #24.* Sponsor track record is computed as of each trial's start date | Using a sponsor's overall stop rate would include the trial being predicted and trials that ended later. |
| 11 | Sponsor name is not a direct feature | Very high cardinality; the point-in-time track record carries the signal without memorising names. |
| 12 | Survival analysis treats completion as a competing event, not as censoring | A completed trial can no longer be terminated. Censoring it and plotting 1 - Kaplan-Meier gives 19.7% terminated by year 5 versus 10.6% from the Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence (current cohort; 19.3% in the first version). Curves use Aalen-Johansen; the Cox model is a cause-specific hazard model, for which censoring completers is correct. |
| 13 | *Partly superseded by #28 (withdrawal is no longer part of the classifier's outcome).* Survival cohort excludes WITHDRAWN and NOT_YET_RECRUITING trials | Neither enrolled anyone and their start date is only a plan, so a "time to stopping" is meaningless. Withdrawal stays in the classifier's outcome. |
| 14 | *Superseded by #30.* In the survival analysis, UNKNOWN trials are censored at their last update and running or suspended trials at the data pull date | The last update is the last day the trial was confirmed to be running. Some UNKNOWN trials have probably stopped quietly, so this censoring is likely informative and slightly understates termination risk. |
| 15 | *Superseded by #30.* Cox hazard ratios are reported from a fit on all start years; the concordance index comes from a separate fit on starts up to 2017 scored on 2018-2019 starts, with the penalty chosen on 2016-2017 | Using every year, including still-running trials, is the reason to do survival analysis; the hold-out fit keeps the performance number honest. |
| 16 | *Superseded by #23.* Isotonic recalibration is fitted on the validation years, which were also used for early stopping | The test years stay untouched. Reusing the validation years makes the calibrator slightly optimistic about the scores it sees, which is small next to the gain from fitting it on years close to the test period. |
| 17 | Subgroups with fewer than 200 trials or fewer than 20 events on the test years are not reported; calibration-in-the-large is computed on recalibrated probabilities | Below that size the AUC confidence interval is too wide to say anything; the recalibrated model is the one that would be used. |
| 18 | Primary outcome: TERMINATED within 5 years of the start date, among trials that actually started. 0 = completed, still running or suspended at the pull, or terminated after year 5 | The old label (terminated or withdrawn vs completed, resolved trials only) dropped trials that were still running. Those are disproportionately long trials that have not stopped, so the stop rate in recent years was inflated (20.4% in 2018-2019). With a fixed horizon every trial in an eligible year has a known label; the rate is roughly flat, 10.6-13.6% per start year across 2008-2020. |
| 19 | "Started" = not WITHDRAWN, not NOT_YET_RECRUITING, and the start date is not marked ESTIMATED | Withdrawn trials never enrolled and their start date is often a plan (2,050 ESTIMATED and 4,004 untyped among 2008-2020 starts; TERMINATED and COMPLETED have none ESTIMATED). Trials whose start type is missing (most records last updated before 2017) are kept: the type field did not exist then, and completed and terminated trials are treated alike. |
| 20 | Only start years 2008-2020 are used for classification | A trial needs 5 years of follow-up before the pull (2026-11-13). 2021 starts before 13 November qualify but the year is incomplete, so it is left out rather than split. |
| 21 | UNKNOWN status trials are excluded from the primary cohort | UNKNOWN means the record was not verified for 2 years, and the last verified status was "ongoing". Counting those verified as running past year 5 as "not terminated" is logically sound but only possible for starts up to about 2019 (the 2-year lapse makes it impossible later), so label coverage would differ between training and test years; it would add only ~1.5% of trials. Both alternatives are run as sensitivity analyses (sensitivity.py). Excluding the 9-19% of trials (depending on start year) that went silent is a limitation: some of them quietly stopped. |
| 22 | Prospective registration = first submitted no later than 21 days after the last possible start day (end of the month for month-only start dates) | The filter's purpose is to drop trials that may have finished before they were registered. For trials that started, the start date is the actual one, and a retrospectively registered trial already knew it, so a rule based on start date alone is not driven by the outcome. What made the old rule outcome-linked was withdrawn trials' planned dates, which are now out of the cohort. The end-of-month rule removes the day-1 artefact of month-only dates (36% of all start dates, almost all before 2017). Dropping the filter, or using the old one, is a sensitivity analysis. A rule based on registration after the end date was rejected: shorter (terminated) trials are more likely to have ended before a late registration, so it would remove positives selectively. |
| 23 | Split by start year: train 2008-2014, early stopping 2015-2016, calibration 2017, test 2018-2020 | Every test year has 5 full years of follow-up, and the isotonic calibrator is fitted on a year that neither training nor early stopping saw. |
| 24 | Sponsor track record: anchored at the earlier of start and first submission; sponsor names normalised (macros/normalise_sponsor.sql); history includes all interventional studies, also those started before 2008; a prior trial counts as "ended" only from 12 months after its end date and only if it was registered before the anchor; separate prior terminated and withdrawn rates | The audit found that for 48% of the prior ended trials counted, the record was last updated after the current trial's start, so the status may not yet have been public. The registry keeps no status history, so a 12-month lag stands in for "the outcome had been posted". Normalisation is deliberately simple (lowercase, punctuation, corporate suffixes, two group names): it merges "Novartis Pharmaceuticals" and "Novartis" but not Genentech and Roche. |
| 25 | Registration delay and start year are not features | The registration delay uses the actual start date, which for registration-time prediction is in the future, and the start year extrapolates under a temporal split. Adding them back raises test AUC only from 0.655 to 0.662 and 0.658 (sensitivity.py). |
| 26 | *Superseded by #34.* Before TF-IDF, sentences that mention stopping, withdrawal, suspension, closure, futility or poor accrual are removed from the text | The registry shows the current text, and some sponsors edit the summary after a trial stops. 22.0% of terminated and 17.5% of other trials lose at least one sentence; the rule over-masks on purpose. |
| 27 | *Superseded by #32.* The headline model includes the text score only if the paired bootstrap interval for its ROC AUC gain excludes zero | Text costs a 6.6 MB model and a preprocessing step in the dashboard; it has to earn its place. After masking the gain is +0.007 (95% CI 0.001 to 0.013), so the text model is the headline model, with the caveat that residual leakage through edited text cannot be ruled out. |
| 28 | Withdrawal before start is a separate secondary analysis, anchored on the registration year | The start date of a withdrawn trial is not a real event, so a time-from-start label does not apply. The question "will a newly registered trial ever start?" uses the same features, split by year of first submission. |
| 29 | The dashboard model is refitted on 2008-2016 with the tree count found by early stopping, recalibrated on 2017 | More recent training data without touching the calibration or test years; the test years still give an honest out-of-time check of the saved model. |
| 30 | Survival cohort uses the same "started" and registration rules as the classifier; UNKNOWN trials are censored at their status verification date; the hold-out is train <= 2017, test 2018-2020, penalty tuned on 2015-2016 | Keeps survival and classification results comparable. The verification date is when the sponsor last confirmed the trial was running, a better censoring time than the date of any edit. |
| 31 | The legacy cohort, label, registration-delay feature and unlagged sponsor history (sponsor_history_v1) are kept in the database | Only to reproduce the earlier models for the leakage ladder (naive -> partly fixed -> defensible); no current model uses them. |
| 32 | The headline model is chosen on 2017 only: both candidates are fitted on 2008-2014 with early stopping on 2015-2016, and the text score is included only if the paired bootstrap interval of its ROC AUC gain on 2017 excludes zero. The test years are scored once, afterwards | Choosing on the test years (as #27 did) makes the headline optimistic. 2017 is also the calibration year; selection and calibration both happen before the test years are touched. On 2017 the gain was +0.0025 (-0.0067 to 0.0118), so the headline is tabular only, although on the test years the text score adds +0.011; the rule was fixed first and is kept. |
| 33 | Out-of-fold text scores on the training years use folds grouped by normalised lead sponsor (StratifiedGroupKFold) | With ungrouped folds the text model can score a trial by recognising its sponsor's other trials (drug code names, house style), so the boosting model learns to over-trust the text score for big sponsors. |
| 34 | Masking removes only sentences in the title and summary that state the study itself stopped, was withdrawn, halted, closed or suspended, under-enrolled, or compare planned and actual numbers (`src/models/text_masking.py`); eligibility criteria are not masked | The broad rule (#26) removed a sentence from 22% of terminated and 17.5% of other trials, mostly innocent ones. The narrow rule hits 0.56% vs 0.11%. Applied to eligibility criteria it fires equally for both outcomes (0.33% vs 0.30%), and what it finds is about participants, so masking criteria would only remove information. |
| 35 | Sensitivity variant: trials labelled 0 only because they are still running are left out when their status was not verified in the 12 months before the pull | 15.1% of 2020 starts are still running and labelled 0 (under 3% in training years); 651 of them (5.4%) are stale. A rule tied to the pull date is the same for every start year, unlike "verified since year 4", which depends on the horizon. It is a sensitivity analysis, not the primary label, because dropping trials by how diligently their sponsors update them is itself a selection. |
| 36 | Bootstrap intervals are reported both over trials and over sponsors (cluster bootstrap on the normalised lead sponsor) | Trials of one sponsor share designs and fates; the trial-level interval treats them as independent and is somewhat too narrow. |
| 37 | Usefulness is shown with a decision curve of the recalibrated headline model (net benefit against flagging all or no trials, thresholds 1-40%) | ROC AUC does not say whether acting on the scores helps; net benefit does, at stated trade-offs between a caught termination and a needless review. |
| 38 | The fixed-horizon label lives in one dbt macro (`macros/terminated_within.sql`) used for the primary label and both UNKNOWN variants | Removes three hand-copied CASE expressions and lets the label be unit-tested on a synthetic table without the database. |
